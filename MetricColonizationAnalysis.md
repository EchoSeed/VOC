# Metric Colonization Analysis

> Trigger this skill when an institution, system, or evaluative framework appears to import measurement constructs from a different domain—especially AI or algorithmic systems—and apply them to human beings, human labor, or human cognition, producing distortions, gaming behaviors, or systematic exclusion of qualitative value.

## Core Thesis
When institutional systems begin assessing humans by output metrics borrowed from AI evaluation frameworks, a profound ontological mismatch emerges: constructs designed to measure machine capability are applied to human beings whose value, cognition, and moral status resist clean quantification. This metric borrowing enacts a form of reductionism that strips tacit knowledge, contextual judgment, and lived experience from evaluation, leaving only what is legible to algorithmic governance. Goodhart's Law activates immediately—once a proxy measure becomes a target, it ceases to accurately represent the construct it was meant to capture, producing metric fixation at the expense of genuine performance. The resulting standardization reflects scalability bias, favoring what can be uniformly processed over what is humanly significant, systematically disadvantaging those whose contributions are qualitative, relational, or culturally particular. Benchmark transfer from AI evaluation carries implicit capability taxonomies that encode the assumptions of their original domain, displacing normative frameworks suited to human dignity and replacing them with technocratic rationality. This constitutes a form of epistemic injustice, as the knowledge and self-understanding of assessed individuals are subordinated to externally imposed, algorithmically legible categories they had no role in constructing. Performativity compounds the harm: people reshape their behavior to satisfy metrics, and the measurement system itself begins producing the subjects it claims merely to describe. Institutional isomorphism accelerates adoption as organizations mimic each other's datafication practices for organizational legitimacy, regardless of ecological validity or fitness for purpose. The deeper structure is one of disciplinary power and biopolitics—human life, labor, and capacity are rendered as data streams amenable to automated decision-making and surveillance capitalism. Distributive justice is compromised when metric-driven systems allocate opportunity, resources, and recognition along lines that systematically misrepresent human-AI alignment problems as solved while leaving structural violence intact. Reflexivity demands we ask who benefits from this incommensurability being obscured, and whether the apparent objectivity of borrowed metrics serves institutional power more than the human beings it purports to evaluate.

## Overview
This skill equips an agent to diagnose and articulate the multi-layered harms that emerge when institutions borrow evaluation frameworks from AI systems and apply them to humans. It operates at the intersection of epistemology, organizational theory, and ethics, tracing how a seemingly neutral act of metric transfer smuggles ontological assumptions, reshapes human behavior through performativity, and enacts epistemic injustice by subordinating human self-understanding to algorithmically legible categories. The skill is not merely descriptive—it generates actionable critique by naming the specific mechanisms (Goodhart's Law, ontological mismatch, normative displacement) through which borrowed metrics degrade the validity of human assessment.

The skill matters because the harms it addresses are structurally invisible: metrics still produce numbers, institutions still appear rigorous, and the displacement of human-appropriate normative frameworks happens without explicit debate. By making these mechanisms legible, the skill enables advocates, designers, and evaluators to intervene before metric fixation solidifies into institutional infrastructure. It is particularly valuable when audit trails, performance management systems, hiring algorithms, or educational credentialing schemes are being designed or reformed.

Reach for this skill whenever you observe persistent anomalies in evaluation outcomes, widespread gaming of performance indicators, systematic disadvantage to qualitative or relational contributors, or when organizational legitimacy is being pursued through datafication rather than genuine fitness-for-purpose. It is also appropriate when stakeholders ask who benefits from a given measurement regime and whether apparent objectivity serves power more than the people being assessed.

## Key Patterns
### Goodhart Activation on Personhood
When AI-borrowed metrics are applied to humans, Goodhart's Law operates not just on performance but on identity itself. People learn to perform measurable competencies while allowing immeasurable ones to atrophy. The metric doesn't merely track the person—it progressively manufactures a new kind of person optimized for legibility. This is qualitatively different from ordinary Goodhart dynamics because the substrate being distorted is human development rather than an organizational KPI.

### Precision Masking Invalidity
Ontological mismatch is routinely undetected because borrowed metrics still produce clean numbers. Institutions mistake the reliability of a measure for its validity, and interrater agreement for accuracy. The quantitative output creates an illusion of rigor that insulates the framework from scrutiny—doubling down on refinement rather than questioning whether the instrument is measuring anything meaningful in the new domain.

### Normative Displacement Without Deliberation
Imported AI metrics quietly redefine what it means to be a good worker, student, or professional without any explicit negotiation. The displacement is complete before stakeholders notice that a normative debate was occurring. This is distinct from explicit policy change: the new framework never argues for itself—it simply makes the old framework unmeasurable and therefore institutionally invisible.

### Benchmark Authority Laundering
Transferring a benchmark from AI evaluation to human contexts carries an implicit authority claim: that whatever the AI benchmark was measuring is the thing that matters in human contexts too. This circular logic borrows the prestige of technical domains associated with innovation and objectivity, pre-emptively delegitimizing qualitative objections as unscientific rather than engaging them on normative grounds.

### Inversion of the Value Alignment Problem
Originally, value alignment asks how to make AI systems pursue human values. When institutions borrow AI evaluation frameworks for humans, the problem inverts: the system now aligns humans to AI-legible values. The alignment problem is not solved—it is exported onto human beings who are expected to reshape themselves to fit the framework's measurement assumptions, at the cost of capacities the framework cannot see.

### Epistemic Injustice Through Metric Imposition
Assessed individuals are subjected to categories they had no role in constructing and which do not honor their self-understanding or the knowledge traditions of their domain. This constitutes a form of testimonial and hermeneutical injustice: not only are their qualitative contributions discounted, but they lack the institutionally recognized vocabulary to contest the framework's adequacy. Reflexivity demands asking who constructed the categories and in whose interests the incommensurability remains obscured.

### Metric Borrowing
**🎯 Decision:** An agent applies metric borrowing when it lacks native evaluation tools and imports measurement frameworks from adjacent domains, typically under pressure to appear rigorous or objective. Institutions adopt AI output metrics for humans when facing accountability demands without having developed human-appropriate alternatives.
**🎭 Analogy:** Using a thermometer to measure happiness: the instrument was built for a different substrate, but its precision creates an illusion of validity that silences doubt.
**💡 Insight:** Metric borrowing is rarely neutral transfer; it smuggles in the ontological assumptions of the source domain. AI metrics assume discrete, repeatable, scorable outputs—importing them into human contexts quietly redefines what a human is supposed to be.

### Ontological Mismatch
**🎯 Decision:** An agent recognizes ontological mismatch when the categories of one system cannot map cleanly onto another without distortion. Apply this concept when evaluation produces persistent anomalies, gaming, or absurdity—signals that the measured entity resists the measuring schema.
**🎭 Analogy:** Trying to weigh a conversation: the scale is real, the reading is real, but the number produced tells you nothing meaningful and everything is lost in translation.
**💡 Insight:** Ontological mismatch often goes undetected precisely because metrics still produce numbers. The mismatch is masked by quantitative output. Institutions may double down on refinement rather than question whether the category applies at all.

### AI Evaluation Frameworks
**🎯 Decision:** An agent deploys AI evaluation frameworks when assessing system performance against defined benchmarks. These frameworks assume the evaluated entity is optimizable, has no interests in the evaluation, and can be decomposed into measurable sub-competencies without remainder.
**🎭 Analogy:** A rubric designed to grade a chess engine being handed to a schoolteacher to grade students: structurally coherent, practically absurd, institutionally irresistible.
**💡 Insight:** AI evaluation frameworks were built for systems with no interiority. Applied to humans, they function not just as measurement tools but as behavioral conditioning systems—reshaping the evaluated toward what can be captured, discarding what cannot.

### Goodhart's Law
**🎯 Decision:** Apply Goodhart's Law when a measure becomes a target and ceases to track what it was meant to represent. Relevant whenever performance incentives are attached to proxy metrics, causing agents to optimize the metric rather than the underlying goal.
**🎭 Analogy:** A hospital rewarded for shorter wait times that begins triaging patients in the parking lot: the number improves; the care does not.
**💡 Insight:** When humans are assessed by AI-borrowed metrics, Goodhart's Law operates on personhood itself. People learn to perform measurable competencies while allowing immeasurable ones to atrophy. The metric doesn't just distort behavior—it restructures identity and aspiration over time.

### Dehumanization
**🎯 Decision:** An agent invokes dehumanization when evaluative or social systems strip away morally relevant human characteristics—interiority, context, dignity, relational complexity—typically by reducing persons to data points or functional roles. Apply when metric systems treat human variance as noise rather than meaning.
**🎭 Analogy:** Replacing a portrait with a barcode: both represent the same person, but one encodes only what the system needs to process them efficiently.
**💡 Insight:** Dehumanization through AI-borrowed metrics is bureaucratic and banal rather than hostile. It requires no malice—only the institutional convenience of treating human beings as if they were the kind of entity that AI metrics were designed to evaluate.

### Value Alignment Problem
**🎯 Decision:** Apply the value alignment problem when an optimizing system pursues measurable proxies that diverge from the values those proxies were meant to represent. Originally an AI safety concern, it applies to human institutions when metric systems systematically reward measurable compliance over genuine human flourishing.
**🎭 Analogy:** Training a dog to wag its tail on command and concluding it is happy: the observable signal is aligned; the underlying state is unknown and irrelevant to the system.
**💡 Insight:** When institutions borrow AI evaluation frameworks for humans, the value alignment problem inverts: instead of asking how to align AI to human values, the system now aligns humans to AI-legible values—a quieter but equally dangerous misalignment.

### Human Assessment
**🎯 Decision:** An agent conducts human assessment to make judgments about a person's capacity, contribution, development, or fit. The challenge is that human assessment inherently involves interpretive judgment about context, potential, and meaning—elements that resist standardization without distortion.
**🎭 Analogy:** A sommelier replaced by a spectrometer: the chemistry is captured precisely; the experience of the wine is not among the variables measured.
**💡 Insight:** Human assessment borrowed from AI frameworks tends to privilege reliability over validity—producing consistent scores for the wrong thing. Institutions mistake interrater agreement for accuracy, not recognizing that evaluators may reliably agree on what is measurable while missing what matters.

### Performativity
**🎯 Decision:** Apply performativity when a measurement or description doesn't merely represent reality but actively produces it. Relevant when evaluated subjects restructure behavior, identity, or presentation to conform to what the evaluation system can see and reward.
**🎭 Analogy:** A map that rewrites the territory: travelers don't navigate to where things are, but to where the map says things should be—and eventually the land follows.
**💡 Insight:** AI-borrowed metrics applied to humans are maximally performative because they are explicit, quantified, and incentivized. They don't observe human performance; they manufacture a new kind of human performance optimized for machine legibility rather than human meaning.

### Metric Fixation
**🎯 Decision:** Apply metric fixation when institutions become so attached to quantified measurement that qualitative judgment is systematically devalued or delegitimized. Decision-makers apply it when numerical outputs displace professional wisdom, contextual interpretation, or ethical deliberation in evaluation processes.
**🎭 Analogy:** A compass worshipped as a god: it was useful for navigation, but now it determines where you must go, regardless of what lies in that direction.
**💡 Insight:** Metric fixation accelerates when AI evaluation frameworks are borrowed because those frameworks carry institutional prestige—they come from domains associated with innovation and objectivity. This prestige makes the fixation harder to question, even as it produces increasingly distorted assessments of human beings.

### Output Metrics
**🎯 Decision:** An agent uses output metrics to assess performance by counting or measuring discrete deliverables—tokens generated, tasks completed, accuracy scores, throughput rates. These metrics are designed for systems where outputs are the only relevant dimension of performance.
**🎭 Analogy:** Judging a chef by plates per hour: the metric is real, the measurement is accurate, and everything essential about cooking is invisible to it.
**💡 Insight:** Output metrics applied to humans create a temporal distortion: they make fast, legible, shallow work appear more valuable than slow, invisible, deep work. This systematically advantages performance types that resemble AI behavior over those that constitute distinctly human contribution.

### Normative Displacement
**🎯 Decision:** Apply normative displacement when a new evaluative framework gradually replaces existing normative standards without explicit debate or democratic deliberation. It occurs when imported metrics quietly redefine what counts as good, competent, or valuable in a domain.
**🎭 Analogy:** A new tenant who never argues but slowly replaces all the furniture: the displacement is complete before anyone noticed a negotiation was happening.
**💡 Insight:** Borrowing AI metrics for human evaluation enacts normative displacement on what it means to be a good worker, student, or professional. The AI framework doesn't just measure performance—it redefines excellence in its own image, making pre-metric human virtues invisible and eventually illegible.

### Benchmark Transfer
**🎯 Decision:** An agent applies benchmark transfer when it uses a validated test from one domain to evaluate performance in another, assuming structural similarity justifies the transfer. The risk is that validity established in the source domain does not automatically carry over.
**🎭 Analogy:** Using a sprinting test to evaluate a surgeon: both involve human bodies, both produce scores, and the scores mean nothing for the purpose at hand.
**💡 Insight:** Benchmark transfer from AI to human evaluation carries a hidden authority claim—that whatever the AI benchmark was measuring is the thing that matters in human contexts too. This circular logic launders contested normative choices as if they were technical decisions.

### Proxy Measure
**🎯 Decision:** An agent uses a proxy measure when the actual target of interest cannot be measured directly. Apply when direct assessment is too costly, subjective, or complex—accepting that the proxy captures some but not all of the target construct.
**🎭 Analogy:** Counting library checkouts to measure community literacy: correlated, convenient, and systematically blind to everything that happens outside the library.
**💡 Insight:** When AI evaluation proxies are applied to humans, the proxy gap tends to be larger than acknowledged, because AI systems were designed to be measurable while humans were not. The proxy doesn't just undercount—it actively misrepresents, treating human complexity as measurement error.

### Tacit Knowledge
**🎯 Decision:** An agent invokes tacit knowledge when recognizing that significant expertise or competence cannot be fully articulated, codified, or transmitted through explicit instruction. Apply when metric systems fail to capture what experienced practitioners know how to do but cannot easily demonstrate on a rubric.
**🎭 Analogy:** Teaching someone to ride a bicycle through a written manual: the instructions are complete; the knowledge is not in them.
**💡 Insight:** AI evaluation frameworks are explicitly hostile to tacit knowledge because they were built for systems that have no tacit dimension—everything a model knows is in principle inspectable. Applying these frameworks to humans systematically devalues and eventually destroys the tacit competencies that constitute the deepest human expertise.

### Structural Violence
**🎯 Decision:** Apply structural violence when harm is produced not by individual actors but by institutional arrangements that systematically disadvantage certain people without visible perpetrators. Relevant when metric systems produce differential harm by design, not intent.
**🎭 Analogy:** A door that fits only one body type: no one chose to exclude, the architect is long gone, but the exclusion is total and daily.
**💡 Insight:** AI-borrowed human evaluation metrics enact structural violence by designing out the competencies, communication styles, and knowledge forms of groups who were never the implicit standard of the AI system. The metric appears neutral; the harm is systematic, invisible, and persistent.

### Quantification
**🎯 Decision:** An agent applies quantification to make complex phenomena tractable, comparable, and actionable by assigning numerical values. The decision involves always sacrificing some richness for manageability, and becomes problematic when the sacrifice is unacknowledged.
**🎭 Analogy:** Translating a poem into a spreadsheet of word frequencies: the translation is accurate; the poem is gone.
**💡 Insight:** Quantifying humans using AI-derived frameworks compounds the standard costs of quantification. AI systems were built to be quantified; humans were not. The categories created for AI legibility—tokens, scores, throughput—impose not just a measurement but an ontology that reshapes what humans are expected to become.

### Human-AI Alignment
**🎯 Decision:** Apply human-AI alignment when designing or auditing systems where AI behavior must conform to human values, intentions, or interests. The core challenge is that human values are implicit, contextual, contradictory, and evolving—hard targets for optimization.
**🎭 Analogy:** Teaching a mirror to reflect not what is there but what the viewer values: technically ambitious, philosophically unstable, and easy to get subtly wrong in ways no one notices.
**💡 Insight:** When institutions reverse the alignment direction—using AI metrics to align humans to AI legibility rather than aligning AI to human values—they invert the ethical logic of the entire alignment project. The danger is no longer an AI that doesn't understand us; it's institutions that no longer want to.
